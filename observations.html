03 / Naturalistic evidence

Longitudinal Observations

A privacy-preserving framework for documenting trajectories, competing explanations and evidence limits.

No private naturalistic record is published in this edition.

This page defines what would be required before any longitudinal observation could be released. Private runtime traces, dialogue excerpts, place names, personal identifiers, internal journals and unreviewed behavioural logs are intentionally withheld.

Publication standard for a longitudinal observation

A public observation should be informative enough to evaluate while remaining independent of private biography.

Minimum methodological context
Predefined observation window, versioned experimental setting, selection rule, eligible events, missingness and relevant system changes.
Evidence separation
Distinguish what was directly recorded from the interpretation placed on it.
Privacy requirement
Use synthetic fixtures or carefully redacted evidence. Remove names, locations, private conversations, personal relationships, identifying timestamps and internal records that are unnecessary for the research claim.
Competing explanations
Record prompt, model, scheduler, environment and researcher-intervention alternatives where relevant.
Allowed claim
A naturalistic trajectory may motivate a hypothesis. It does not establish a persistence mechanism without a controlled comparison.

Observation slot 001 / Withheld pending anonymisation

No public source trace attached

No concrete behaviour, event history or private character detail is published here. If a naturalistic case is released later, the public record will contain only the minimum redacted material required to assess a clearly bounded research claim.

Temporal persistence

Does an effect remain measurable after the immediate context disappears?

B

Cross-context generalisation

Does a change appear outside the context in which it was first induced?

C

Revision

Does later contradictory evidence appropriately reduce or redirect a prior influence?

Naturalistic observation ≠ controlled causal evidence

A documented trajectory can establish what was recorded in a bounded setting. Attributing that trajectory to a mechanism requires a controlled comparison. See the proposed studies.
