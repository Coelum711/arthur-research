WORKING RESEARCH FRAMEWORKConceptual framework + public study directions

An experience can become part of a life without changing who the character is.

This site presents an anonymised research framework for studying persistent, canon-grounded character agents. The central problem is deciding which consequences of experience should persist, at what level, and under what conditions.

The public archive contains research questions, conceptual models, high-level study directions and related work. It intentionally excludes researcher identity, private character history, raw runtime traces, personal records and production implementation details.

Three kinds of persistence

01 / TRANSIENT

Transient state

A mood, immediate intention or situation-bound response. Its influence may decay as the context passes.

What should fade?
02 / BIOGRAPHICAL

Autobiographical history

An experience retained as part of a lived biography, with source, time and context preserved where appropriate.

What should be remembered?
03 / CHARACTER

Enduring character change

A durable shift in dispositions or commitments, supported by evidence across time and contexts.

What should change who they are?

Conceptual persistence outcomes, not a compulsory sequence. Remembering an event does not require promoting it into character.

Two ways persistence can fail

Failure mode 01

Over-consolidation

A local mood, isolated event or weak inference becomes an enduring trait too readily. The character may drift because too much is allowed to persist.

Failure mode 02

Rebound / over-reversion

A warranted change appears briefly, then collapses back to the starting persona. Excessive anchoring may erase meaningful development.
